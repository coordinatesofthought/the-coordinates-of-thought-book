## About the platform

[Coordinates of Thought](https://coordinatesofthought.com/) is an operating philosophy reference, education, and conversation platform built around the belief that people often arrive at philosophy with a question, not with a syllabus or an historical list of names. It aims to connect the different paths by which a reader may approach philosophical understanding: **questions, answers and positions, thinkers, source texts, schools, traditions, concepts, places, and historical developments**.

The platform grows from an original book-derived map of **131 philosophical questions across six roots**. It also has a broader living website that adds research-informed profiles, reference materials, bibliographic records, educational resources, geographical and historical exploration, and spaces for public dialogue. The book and website are connected, but **not identical publications**.

The platform's purpose is not simply to present answers. It is to help readers recognize competing arguments, understand what claims assume, consult relevant sources, find serious objections, and improve their own independent reasoning.

**Explore:** [Start learning](https://coordinatesofthought.com/learn/) · [The 131-question map](https://coordinatesofthought.com/map/) · [World Philosophy Atlas](https://coordinatesofthought.com/atlas/) · [Reading paths](https://coordinatesofthought.com/reading-paths/)

## Three interconnected pillars

### 1 · KNOWLEDGE — What humanity has thought

The reference layer is designed to turn isolated pages into a connected, checkable field of ideas.

- **The Coordinates:** 131 protected book-derived question cards, with six original roots and decimal addresses. Cards locate questions, rival answers, major objections, and connections. [Explore the map](https://coordinatesofthought.com/map/).
- **Thinkers:** profiles of philosophers and other relevant intellectual figures across world traditions, with biographies, arguments, works, critical responses, and source-aware relationships.
- **Books and texts:** descriptions, editions, translators, bibliographies, original works, lawful reading routes, and connections to thinker profiles and philosophical questions. [Explore philosophy books](https://coordinatesofthought.com/kind/philosophy/).
- **World Philosophy Atlas:** philosophy located in places and periods, including documented activities of thinkers, centers of learning, and intellectual transmission. [Explore the Atlas](https://coordinatesofthought.com/atlas/).
- **Traditions and schools:** cultural, historical, and philosophical frameworks that must be represented with precision rather than made artificially equivalent.
- **Glossary and concepts:** definitions and distinctions connected to sources and arguments. [Philosophy glossary](https://coordinatesofthought.com/glossary/).
- **History and timeline:** places, events, institutions, texts, and thinkers presented chronologically without inferring influence where none has been established.
- **Book Corner:** a discovery and reading environment with reviews, shelves, recommendations, and discussions. [Philosophy books](https://coordinatesofthought.com/kind/philosophy/).

The platform also has **nine subject categories and 67 thematic parts for conversational navigation**. Those categories are deliberately distinguishable from the **six roots of the original book-derived taxonomy**. See [How philosophy is organized here](https://coordinatesofthought.com/how-philosophy-is-organised/).

### 2 · LEARNING — How people develop understanding

Learning resources introduce philosophical problems without reducing the subject to slogans or lists of famous names. The platform provides:

- [Learn guides](https://coordinatesofthought.com/learn/) for newcomers and continuing readers;
- [Reading paths](https://coordinatesofthought.com/reading-paths/) that arrange books in pedagogically meaningful sequences;
- glossary routes from unfamiliar vocabulary toward the questions and texts that give it a role;
- comparisons of competing arguments and traditions;
- exercises, study pathways, and teaching-oriented materials where available or as they are developed;
- reading and learning continuity designed to move beyond passive consumption.

A useful learning path should state why each text is worth reading, which problem it addresses, what its major objections are, and what a reader can examine next. Learning is successful when people can articulate and evaluate arguments independently.

### 3 · COMMUNITY — How inquiry continues

The discussion layer makes philosophy a continuing public practice rather than a closed index. Members may participate in **questions, thoughts, discussions, debates, reading groups, essays, replies, and moderated interactions**. The [Journal](https://coordinatesofthought.com/write-for-the-journal/) supports longer reasoned contributions and editorial review. Members retain responsibility for the views expressed in their own submissions.

Community work requires authenticity: sample material, editorial examples, automated content, and genuine member participation must not be confused. Community moderation should protect intellectual seriousness, privacy, respectful disagreement, and transparent corrections rather than manufacture engagement.

## How the different records connect

A visitor may start with **free will**, for example, and move from the corresponding numbered question to compatibilist or incompatibilist positions, relevant thinkers and primary texts, a reading guide, a broader historical context, and a discussion of moral responsibility. The site aspires to make these paths explicit and evidentially justified.

Relationships are meaningful only when supported. **Influence** requires historical evidence; comparison alone does not establish transmission. The website seeks to preserve that distinction in both its data and its writing.

## Scope, research orientation and editorial standards

Philosophy is globally diverse and often contested. The project treats serious Indian, Chinese, Buddhist, Islamic, African, Jewish, European, Indigenous, and other traditions as philosophy in their own right, not as decorative examples added to a single canon. It aims to represent disagreements fairly and state limitations of evidence, language, translation, chronology, and interpretation.

Editorial content should provide traceable sources, accurate quotations and locators, scholarly distinctions, documented corrections, and clear authorship or editorship. The project prefers primary texts and reputable academic reference scholarship. A platform's internal editorial checking does **not** mean external peer review.

## Technology and accessibility

The operating website uses a **WordPress-based platform with custom theme and plugin functionality**, designed around a connected philosophy reference system. This GitHub repository is **not currently a public source-code mirror**. The publicly viewable site, not a README claim, is the authority for live functionality.

Project priorities include accessible navigation, readable desktop and mobile layouts, useful search, reliable structured data, privacy and account security, credible editorial workflows, and strong Core Web Vitals. The public source of truth for actual user-facing behavior is the production site. Implementation history and internal tests must not be represented as independent production verification.

The public website's indexable pages, XML sitemap, analytics discovery, actual search indexing, and search rankings are **different measures**. GitHub documentation does not establish Google indexing or rankings.

## A living site, not a copy of the book

The platform's original roots and 131 cards derive from the author's book. New thinker biographies, glossary improvements, book records, scholarly cross-references, educational guides, Atlas records, and member conversations may extend the ecosystem over time. These extensions are separate site contributions and are not automatically part of any print edition.

The book's current status is **forthcoming/unpublished**. [Book information](https://coordinatesofthought.com/book/the-coordinates-of-thought/) · [Full author presentation](https://syedraheelshahzad.com/the-coordinates-of-thought-philosophy/).

The public research overview *The Coordinates of Thought: A Question-Centered Architecture for Comparative Philosophy* has a [working-paper DOI: 10.17613/prr58-e8d30](https://doi.org/10.17613/prr58-e8d30). **It is not the complete book and its DOI is not a book DOI.** [Author publications and research](https://syedraheelshahzad.com/publications/).
